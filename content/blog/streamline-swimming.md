A strong streamline is the foundation of efficient swimming. It teaches your body how to hold tension, stay narrow, and move forward without wasted motion.

## Why Streamline Matters

A streamline is a locked, high-tension body line that helps you knife through the water with minimal drag. It gives immediate feedback: if one part of the body leaks out of position, you slow down right away.

When swimmers practice streamline consistently, they improve:

- Body alignment
- Core connection
- Glide distance
- Stroke efficiency

## Build the Position Step by Step

1. Stack one hand over the other.
2. Wrap the top thumb around the bottom hand to lock the shape.
3. Reach both arms long overhead.
4. Squeeze biceps behind the ears and keep elbows tight.
5. Engage core and glutes to hold one connected line.
6. Keep legs straight, together, and toes pointed.

## Common Drag Leaks to Watch

- Hands separating during glide
- Elbows drifting wide
- Head lifting too high
- Knees bending or legs splitting

Spotting and fixing these leaks is the fastest way to improve distance per glide.

## Training Set: 5 x Streamline Glides

Try this simple set at the start of practice:

- Push off from the wall (or bottom in open water)
- Hold streamline with **no kicks**
- Exhale steadily until breath runs out
- Walk or easy swim back to start
- Repeat for 5 total reps

Track your best glide distance each session. Small gains over time usually translate to cleaner technique across all strokes.

## Coaching Cue

Think: **"Long, locked, and quiet."**

If the body stays long, the core stays locked, and the water around you stays quiet, you are likely in a strong streamline.
